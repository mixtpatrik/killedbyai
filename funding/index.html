All 37 failed AI companies

#1 GM Cruise

$10B

The robotaxi startup GM bought in 2016 and then poured more than $10 billion into, with Honda ($852M), Microsoft and SoftBank alongside. Driverless paid rides began in San Francisco in 2022; a 2023 crash in which a Cruise car dragged a pedestrian, and the company's handling of it, cost it its California permit. In December 2024 Mary Barra ended the program, citing the 'incremental time and capital' still needed. The most expensive AI product death on record.

Product killed 2013–Dec 2024 · 11.9 yrs $2.3M/day Killed by General Motors Source ↗

#2 Argo AI

$3.6B

Pittsburgh's self-driving champion, backed by roughly $1 billion from Ford and $2.6 billion from Volkswagen. Six years in, both backers decided that fully driverless cars were further away than they could keep paying for, declined to invest further, and absorbed its assets. Around 2,000 employees were offered jobs at the two carmakers; the company itself simply stopped.

Startup failed 2016–Oct 2022 · 5.9 yrs $1.7M/day Killed by Ford & Volkswagen Source ↗

#3 Inflection AI / Pi

$1.5B

A 'personal AI' focused on emotional intelligence and empathy, backed by $1.5B in funding. Effectively killed when Microsoft hired CEO Mustafa Suleyman and most of the engineering team, leaving Pi as an orphaned product.

Acqui-hired 2023–Mar 2024 · 0.9 yrs $4.7M/day Killed by Microsoft Source ↗

#4 Olive AI

$902M

A healthcare AI startup once valued at $4 billion that promised to automate hospital admin tasks. Despite raising $902M, investigations revealed its 'AI' relied heavily on manual human labor behind the scenes. After mass layoffs, it shut down and sold its assets for a fraction of its valuation.

Startup failed 2017–Oct 2023 · 6.8 yrs $362K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#5 Forward (CarePods)

$657M

A primary-care startup that raised $657M on the promise of AI-run doctor's offices, culminating in 'CarePods' — self-serve kiosks meant to automate check-ups. Only three pods ever launched, one was pulled soon after installation, and former staff described patients getting stuck inside. Forward shut down in November 2024 with a few weeks' notice.

Startup failed 2016–Nov 2024 · 8.9 yrs $203K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#6 Builder.ai

$445M

Microsoft-backed 'AI app builder' valued at $1.5B. Its 'neural network' turned out to be 700 engineers in India writing code manually. After inflating revenue 300% ($55M real vs $220M claimed), a creditor seized $37M from its accounts, triggering bankruptcy across five countries.

Startup failed 2016–May 2025 · 9.4 yrs $130K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#7 Adept AI (Independent)

$415M

An AI startup building autonomous agents that could use software tools, valued at $1B with $415M raised. Amazon hired 80% of the team including the CEO and co-founders — a 'reverse acqui-hire' that drew FTC scrutiny as a pattern of Big Tech absorbing AI startups without triggering antitrust review.

Acqui-hired 2022–Jun 2024 · 2.5 yrs $457K/day Killed by Amazon Source ↗

#8 Embark Trucks

$400M

An autonomous-trucking company that went public via SPAC in 2021 and raised more than $400 million. With no revenue and capital markets closed to pre-revenue companies, it cut 70% of staff and began liquidating its self-driving truck assets in March 2023. Its timeline for driverless freight never arrived; the money did run out.

Startup failed 2016–Mar 2023 · 7.2 yrs $153K/day Killed by Market Source ↗

#9 Humane AI Pin

$230M

The $699 screenless AI wearable that was supposed to replace smartphones. Plagued by overheating, terrible battery life, and painfully slow responses. Humane sought a buyer after disastrous reviews and abysmal sales.

Hardware discontinued 2024–May 2025 · 1.1 yrs $597K/day Killed by Reality Source ↗

#10 Ghost Autonomy

$220M

A self-driving software startup that raised about $220M and, months before closing, landed an investment from OpenAI's startup fund to bring large language models to autonomous driving. It shut down worldwide on April 3, 2024, unable to secure the long-term funding its plan required.

Startup failed 2017–Apr 2024 · 7.3 yrs $83K/day Killed by Market Source ↗

#11 Untether AI

$168M

A Toronto AI-inference chip startup selling speedAI accelerators and the imAIgine SDK. PitchBook data cited by The Logic puts its funding at US$168M from backers including CPP Investments, Intel Capital and Radical Ventures, with the last major round in July 2021. In early June 2025 AMD took its engineering team in an undisclosed deal, and Untether said it would 'no longer be supplying or supporting our speedAI products and imAIgine software development kit.' The engineers went to work on AMD's AI compiler, kernel and chip-design efforts.

Acqui-hired 2018–Jun 2025 · 7.4 yrs $62K/day Killed by AMD Source ↗

#12 Character.AI (Independent)

$150M

The viral AI chatbot platform where users created and talked to AI 'characters.' After safety controversies and intense competition, Google effectively acqui-hired the founders and key staff, leaving the company a shell of its former self.

Acqui-hired 2022–Aug 2024 · 1.9 yrs $219K/day Killed by Google Source ↗

#13 Soul Machines

$135M

A New Zealand maker of AI 'Digital People' avatars, co-founded in 2016 by Greg Cross and Mark Sagar, a two-time Sci-Tech Academy Award winner for facial-capture work at Weta Digital. It raised more than US$135M (about NZ$225M) from backers including Horizons Ventures, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Salesforce Ventures and Temasek. On February 5, 2026 KPMG's Leon Bowker and Luke Norman were appointed receivers. On September 28, 2026 AppDirect announced it had acquired Soul Machines on undisclosed terms and would build the avatars into Devs.ai, so the faces live on with a different employer.

Startup failed 2016–Feb 2026 · 10.1 yrs $37K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#14 Jasper AI (Original Platform)

$125M

One of the first AI writing unicorns, valued at $1.5B. Jasper's 'wrapper' business model collapsed as GPT-4o and Claude made its core features available for free or at a fraction of the cost.

Startup failed 2021–Jun 2025 · 4.4 yrs $78K/day Killed by OpenAI / Anthropic Source ↗

#15 Woebot

$114M

A pioneering AI therapy chatbot used by 1.5 million people that delivered cognitive behavioral therapy via smartphone. Despite FDA Breakthrough Device designation, the company shut down because the regulatory process for AI mental health tools proved too costly and slow as LLMs outpaced the FDA's ability to regulate them.

Startup failed 2017–Jun 2025 · 8.1 yrs $39K/day Killed by FDA Source ↗

#16 Stability AI (Stable Diffusion Creator)

$101M

The company behind Stable Diffusion, the open-source model that democratized AI image generation. After CEO Emad Mostaque resigned amid financial turmoil and copyright lawsuits, the company struggled to survive as a going concern.

Startup failed 2020–Jun 2024 · 4.4 yrs $63K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#17 Writer (GPT Wrapper Era)

$100M

Originally an AI writing assistant for enterprises built on GPT. Pivoted multiple times as the 'wrapper' model died, eventually trying to become a full AI platform. Struggled as enterprises adopted native Copilot and Gemini integrations.

Startup failed 2020–Jan 2025 · 5.0 yrs $55K/day Killed by Microsoft Copilot Source ↗

#18 Neeva

$78M

An ad-free, AI-powered search engine founded by ex-Google executives. Despite raising $77.5M, it couldn't compete with free alternatives. Acquired by Snowflake for its AI talent, and the search engine was shut down.

Acqui-hired 2019–May 2023 · 4.4 yrs $48K/day Killed by Snowflake Source ↗

#19 Esperanto Technologies

$63M

A RISC-V AI-inference chipmaker founded in 2014 by Transmeta co-founder Dave Ditzel. Its 7nm ET-SoC-1 packed 1,088 small ET-Minion cores and four larger ET-Maxion cores onto one chip. A $58M Series B in November 2018 brought equity funding to $63M, plus a $1.5M PPP loan in 2020. In July 2025 it closed most operations, keeping only the staff needed to sell its IP, after rivals offered prospective employees up to four times its pay. Ainekko (Nekko.ai) bought the IP that October and is open-sourcing it.

Startup failed 2014–Jul 2025 · 11.5 yrs $15K/day Killed by Deep-Pocketed Rivals Source ↗

#20 Moxie Robot

$54M

An $800 AI social robot designed to help children develop emotional skills, particularly popular with autistic kids. When funding collapsed, the company shut down abruptly — bricking all devices and leaving heartbroken children saying goodbye to their companion on camera. No refunds were offered.

Hardware discontinued 2020–Dec 2024 · 4.2 yrs $35K/day Killed by Embodied Inc. Source ↗

#21 Robin AI

$44M

UK legal tech startup that raised $44M to build AI-powered contract review tools used by PwC and KPMG. After failing to close a $50M Series C, the company cut a third of its staff and was listed on an insolvency marketplace — just 9 months after making the Sunday Times 100 Tech list.

Startup failed 2019–Nov 2025 · 6.8 yrs $18K/day Killed by Market Source ↗

#22 Yupp.ai

$33M

A crowdsourced AI-evaluation platform that paid users to compare model outputs, backed by $33M from a16z crypto. It reached 1.3 million users and still died inside a year. The labs had moved on to expert-sourced data and agentic evaluation — broad consumer preference signal simply stopped being the scarce input anyone would pay for. Co-founders Pankaj Gupta and Gilad Mishne kept it accessible for two weeks after the announcement, then closed signups permanently.

Startup failed 2025–Mar 2026 · 0.8 yrs $109K/day Killed by Market Source ↗

#23 Limitless Pendant (Rewind AI)

$33M

A $99 AI wearable pendant that recorded and transcribed in-person conversations, previously known as Rewind. Meta acquired the company and immediately ended hardware sales, sunset the desktop app, and cut off EU/UK service entirely due to GDPR concerns.

Hardware discontinued 2024–Dec 2025 · 1.6 yrs $57K/day Killed by Meta Source ↗

#24 Kintsugi AI

$30M

An AI startup that spent 7 years and $30M developing voice biomarker technology to detect depression and anxiety from how people speak. Shut down after nearly 4 years waiting for FDA De Novo clearance that never came. Open-sourced all code and research before closing — a rare act of scientific integrity in an industry of quiet deaths.

Startup failed 2019–Feb 2026 · 7.1 yrs $12K/day Killed by FDA Source ↗

#25 Rabbit R1

$30M

A $199 'AI-native' gadget designed to replace apps with a 'Large Action Model.' Widely panned as a repackaged Android app with a $199 price tag. The hardware became essentially abandoned after the company pivoted to software-only.

Hardware discontinued 2024–Mar 2025 · 0.9 yrs $96K/day Killed by Reality Source ↗

#26 PlayAI (PlayHT)

$21M

PlayHT began in 2016 as a browser extension that read articles aloud and grew into a text-to-speech, voice-cloning and voice-agent platform, later renamed PlayAI. Founders Hammad Syed and Mahmoud Felfel went through Y Combinator and raised a $21M seed round in November 2024 led by Kindred Ventures and 500 Global. In July 2025 Meta acquired the company and took the entire team to work on voice for Meta AI, AI Characters and wearables. The API was given until December 31, 2025, and voice-agent frameworks such as Pipecat deprecated their PlayHT integrations ahead of the cutoff.

Acqui-hired 2016–Dec 2025 · 10.0 yrs $6K/day Killed by Meta Source ↗

#27 Tune AI

$20M

Backed by Accel and Flipkart, this GenAI platform offered LLM fine-tuning tools via Tune Chat and Tune Studio. Killed when major cloud providers released identical tooling at lower cost, and most users were free developers who never converted to paying customers.

Startup failed 2018–Jun 2025 · 7.4 yrs $7K/day Killed by AWS / Google / Azure Source ↗

#28 Relay.app

$8M

AI-agent workflow automation pitched as a Zapier alternative, founded by Jacob Bank — ex-Google Gmail and Calendar product lead whose previous startup Timeful was bought by Google. On July 16, 2026 the homepage was simply replaced with 'Relay.app is shutting down,' with no reason given. A month later the answer arrived: Bank and part of the team joined Google's Chrome division. The free tier stopped working August 15; paid accounts were given until September 14.

Acqui-hired 2021–Jul 2026 · 5.5 yrs $4K/day Killed by Google Source ↗

#29 theGist

$7M

An Israeli startup that scanned Slack, email, CRM and analytics to hand each employee a personalised summary of what mattered — founded in 2022 by alumni of Wix, Blizzard, OpenWeb and Gong, with a $7M pre-seed co-led by StageOne and Aleph. A pivot toward sales tooling never caught, and rather than spend the remainder the founders closed the company and said they would return the money to investors. ⚠ Closure announced September 3, 2025 (Calcalist/CTech); no exact shutdown date was published.

Startup failed 2022–Sep 2025 · 3.7 yrs $5K/day Killed by Itself Source ↗

#30 Huxe

$5M

An AI audio app from three former NotebookLM developers — prompt it and get a podcast, a personalised feed, or a live AI radio station you could interrupt mid-sentence. It raised $4.6M from Conviction, Dylan Field and Google's own Jeff Dean. Spotify shipped a near-identical personal podcast feature one day before the wind-down notice, and Google's Gemini Daily Brief had landed at I/O weeks earlier. Built by ex-Googlers, buried by Google.

Startup failed 2024–May 2026 · 1.6 yrs $8K/day Killed by Google / Spotify Source ↗

#31 Weights (weights.gg)

$4M

The community voice-cloning and AI-music platform where thousands of celebrity and character voice models lived. Weights announced in January 2026 that it had never been profitable and closed on March 31; two months later The New York Times reported that OpenAI had quietly bought its technology and roughly six engineers. Its library of consent-free celebrity voices had become a liability as deepfake laws tightened — the thing that made it popular was the thing that made it unsellable as a product.

Acqui-hired 2023–Mar 2026 · 2.8 yrs $4K/day Killed by OpenAI Source ↗

#32 Reforged Labs

$4M

Built AI-generated video ad creative for mobile game studios and won real customers — Supercell, King, Ubisoft, Playtika, Scopely — with 17 staff across Vietnam and the US. CEO Robert Huynh shut it down after three years because foundation models got good enough for studios to build the same tooling in-house: 'the gap we were selling into is closing.' Its biggest customers signed six-figure contracts; there were simply too few studios at that scale to sustain a company.

Startup failed 2023–Jul 2026 · 3.6 yrs $3K/day Killed by Foundation Models Source ↗

#33 Stainless SDK Generator

$4M

The startup that auto-generated production-ready API SDKs in Python, TypeScript, Go, Java, and Kotlin. Used by OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare, Replicate, Runway, and Meta. Anthropic acquired it for $300M+ and immediately announced it would wind down all hosted products — a strategic kill to deny competitors access to critical infrastructure.

Acqui-hired 2022–May 2026 · 4.4 yrs $2K/day Killed by Anthropic Source ↗

#34 Coqui AI

$3M

An open-source text-to-speech startup that built the popular Coqui TTS library. Despite being a go-to for developers wanting voice synthesis, the company ran out of funding and shut down, releasing its final models to the community before going dark.

Startup failed 2021–Mar 2024 · 3.2 yrs $3K/day Killed by Market Source ↗

#35 Yara AI

$2M

UK-based AI mental health companion offering CBT-style exercises and conversational support. The founder voluntarily shut it down after deciding AI chatbots aren't safe enough for people in real mental health crises — a rare case of ethical self-termination.

Startup failed 2024–Nov 2025 · 1.9 yrs $3K/day Killed by Its Own Founder Source ↗

#36 Rilo

$1M

An Indian startup founded in 2025 by IIT Bombay alumni Dhruv Jaglan and Georgi Boby, selling AI agents for marketing work described in plain English: competitor intelligence, campaign research, social outreach, content repurposing and sales-call analysis. It raised $1M from Peak XV Partners, DeVC and Day Zero Ventures at a $10M valuation and, by Jaglan's account, reached 10,000 users within months of launch. On September 2, 2026 Adobe acquired it in an undisclosed deal that licensed its technology and brought its six-person team into Adobe. The platform shut down after the deal; no switch-off date was published.

Acqui-hired 2025–Sep 2026 · 1.7 yrs $2K/day Killed by Adobe Source ↗

#37 Cydoc

$500K

A bootstrapped health AI startup that operated for 7 years trying to build clinical AI tools. The founder published a detailed postmortem explaining why even technically sound health AI products die: the regulatory maze, slow hospital sales cycles, and the impossibility of competing with Big Tech's free AI offerings.
